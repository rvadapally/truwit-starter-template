Cryptographic Proof in Three Steps

No technical knowledge required

📤

Step 1: Upload or Link

What you do: Drag & drop a file or paste a URL from YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, or X.

What happens: We securely download or reference your content without storing it permanently.

Privacy First: We never keep your media files. They're deleted after verification.

🔐

Step 2: Cryptographic Signing

What we do: Compute a SHA-256 hash (content fingerprint), attach C2PA metadata, and sign it with Ed25519.

Why it matters: This creates a tamper-proof record that mathematically proves the content existed at this moment in time.

Can't be faked: The signature is mathematically impossible to forge without our private key.

Step 3: Immutable Proof

What you get: A trustmark ID, embeddable badge, and public verification page.

How to use it: Share the verification link, embed the badge on your website, or show it to clients/platforms.

Forever yours: Your proof persists indefinitely. No one can delete or alter it.

Technical Deep Dive

For the technically curious

What is C2PA?

C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) is an open standard developed by Adobe, Microsoft, The New York Times, and others. It defines how to embed cryptographic metadata directly into media files, creating a tamper-evident record of content creation and modifications. Truwit implements C2PA to ensure our proofs are interoperable with other tools in the content authenticity ecosystem.

How does cryptographic signing work?

We use Ed25519, a public-key signature system. Here's how it works: We have a private key (kept secret on our servers) and a public key (available to anyone). When we sign your content's hash with our private key, we create a signature that can only be verified with the matching public key. Anyone can verify the signature is authentic, but only we can create it. This proves the content hasn't been altered since we signed it.

Where is the data stored?

We store three things: (1) the SHA-256 hash of your content, (2) optional metadata you choose to attach, and (3) the cryptographic signature. We do NOT store the actual media file—it's deleted immediately after hashing. The hash is a one-way function, meaning it's mathematically impossible to reverse it back to your original content. Your data is stored in a PostgreSQL database hosted on Railway with enterprise-grade security.

Can proofs be faked?

No. The Ed25519 signature can only be created with our private key, which never leaves our secure servers. Even if someone tried to claim they created a proof, they couldn't produce a valid signature. Additionally, our proofs include timestamps and are stored immutably—no one, not even us, can alter them after creation. The only way to "fake" a proof would be to steal our private key, which is protected by multiple layers of security and would be immediately detected.
